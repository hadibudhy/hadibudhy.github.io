# Methodology and evidence rules

1. Define the decision owner, primary KPI, guardrails, and unit of analysis before inspecting results.
2. Validate row counts, schema, missingness, duplicates, date coverage, and join cardinality.
3. Use intention-to-treat for randomized assignment. Treat exposure as downstream unless a defensible instrument exists.
4. Report absolute effects, relative effects, confidence intervals, p-values, and practical thresholds together.
5. Label exploratory segment findings and correct for multiple comparisons.
6. Treat recorded dispatched trips as a fulfilled-trip proxy, not total demand, unless request and lost-match data are present.
7. Use event studies and difference-in-differences only after checking pre-trends, placebo dates, composition, anticipation, and spillovers.
8. Quantify scenarios only from explicit assumptions. Never present a scenario as observed revenue or profit.
9. Prefer a null or unidentified result over a causal claim that the data cannot support.
